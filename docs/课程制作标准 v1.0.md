**Scope:** All 11 Stages, 61 lessons  
**Authority:** Product Owner (PO)

## 1. Product Positioning

MarketPilot is a comprehensive financial-market learning platform.

Its central subject is finance and financial markets. Complex systems theory serves as an explanatory framework, not an independent encyclopedia.

All lessons must follow three principles:

- **Narrative-Driven:** Use questions, stories, historical events, real-world situations, or puzzles to drive learning.
- **Intuition-First:** Establish an intuitive understanding before introducing technical terminology and formal models.
- **Mechanism-Rigorous:** Explain causal mechanisms accurately, preserve academic boundaries, and verify important claims.

The goal is to make difficult financial knowledge understandable without sacrificing intellectual depth.

## 2. Fixed Teaching Standards

### 2.1 Language

- Use natural, readable Chinese.
- Prefer direct sentences over unnecessarily long academic prose.
- Introduce professional terminology with its English equivalent when appropriate.
- Explain unfamiliar concepts before relying on them.
- Use concrete examples and analogies to clarify abstract mechanisms.
- Explain where an analogy stops being accurate.
- Avoid unnecessary jargon, artificial humor, and repetitive disclaimers.
- Do not oversimplify a concept to the point of inaccuracy.

### 2.2 Teaching Method

Prefer the following teaching progression when appropriate:

1. Introduce a meaningful question.
2. Establish an intuitive understanding.
3. Introduce necessary concepts.
4. Explain the underlying mechanism.
5. Demonstrate through examples, evidence, or calculations.
6. Discuss limitations and exceptions.
7. Connect the conclusion to the broader financial system.

This is a logical framework, NOT a mandatory chapter template.

### 2.3 Lesson Length

Standard teaching-body length: **5,000–8,000 Chinese characters**.

Preferred target: **6,000–7,000 characters**.

Counting rules:
- Count Chinese characters in the teaching body.
- Exclude punctuation, numbers, English words, titles, headings, diagrams, interactive elements, exercises, answers, references, and internal production notes.
- Do not add filler merely to reach the target.

Exceptional lessons may fall outside the standard range when educational requirements justify it. Explain the reason and obtain PO approval.

## 3. Academic and Evidence Standards

### 3.1 Claim Classification

Important claims should be distinguishable as:

- Historical facts
- Financial or institutional mechanisms
- Model results under explicit assumptions
- Interpretations or disputed explanations

### 3.2 Source Priority

Prefer:

1. Official primary sources and original legal or regulatory documents.
2. Official statistical databases and institutional publications.
3. Original academic papers and high-quality academic research.
4. Reliable secondary sources when primary evidence is unavailable.

Never represent secondary evidence as primary evidence.

### 3.3 Verification Labels

| Label | Meaning | Publication treatment |
|---|---|---|
| ✔ | Relevant original evidence verified | May be used |
| ◐ | Partial, secondary, or incomplete evidence | Qualify wording or remove |
| ☐ | Not verified | Cannot be presented as established fact |

These labels describe verification of the specific claim, not the overall credibility of a source.

### 3.4 Required Practices

- Important factual claims must be traceable to sources.
- Statistics must identify dates, units, and definitions.
- Mathematical results must identify assumptions and calculation methods.
- Regulatory claims must specify jurisdiction and applicable scope.
- Historical interpretations must distinguish documented facts from scholarly explanations.
- Unresolved claims must be disclosed internally.
- Remove unsupported claims rather than inventing explanations.

Academic rigor should be reflected in precise writing, not excessive disclaimers.

## 4. Mathematical and Numerical Verification

Every numerical claim must be correct and reproducible.

Verification methods depend on complexity:

| Complexity | Minimum verification |
|---|---|
| Simple arithmetic | Manual calculation and independent recalculation |
| Multi-step formulas | Independent calculation and formula verification |
| Financial models | Numerical script and independent result |
| Scenario simulations | Script tests and boundary tests |
| Interactive calculations | Data assertions and browser tests |

JavaScript verification scripts are NOT mandatory for every lesson.

Use the simplest method that provides sufficient confidence.

Check, where applicable:

- Numerator and denominator
- Units and time periods
- Annualization conventions
- Compounding frequency
- Rounding
- Data dates and definitions
- Model assumptions
- Boundary conditions

## 5. Lesson Structure and Flexibility

There is no mandatory number of chapters.

Opening formats may include:

- A personal or historical story
- A real financial event
- A counterintuitive question
- A numerical puzzle
- A thought experiment
- A market phenomenon

Chapter order and depth must follow the subject.

Different financial topics require different approaches:

- Banking: balance sheets and accounting flows
- Fixed income: pricing, yields, and mathematical derivation
- Financial history: events, institutions, and historical evidence
- Market microstructure: order books and execution simulations
- Monetary policy: causal transmission mechanisms
- Complex systems: feedback loops, networks, and emergent behavior

Do not force a lesson into an unsuitable template.

## 6. Visuals and Interactivity

Visual elements must serve an identifiable educational purpose.

Possible formats include:

- Diagrams
- Historical photographs
- Charts
- Tables
- Step-by-step illustrations
- Interactive calculations
- Financial simulations
- Decision exercises

No fixed number of visuals or interactions is required.

Accessibility requirements:

- Support keyboard interaction where applicable.
- Adapt to desktop and mobile layouts.
- Provide static alternatives for important animated content.
- Respect reduced-motion preferences.
- Ensure essential information is not available only through animation.

Avoid decorative interaction without educational value.

## 7. Cross-Lesson Consistency

Maintain consistency across all 61 lessons.

Requirements:

- Unified terminology and definitions.
- Consistent fictional characters and institutions.
- Consistent mathematical notation and units.
- Clear ownership of concepts across lessons.
- Appropriate references to prerequisite lessons.
- No unnecessary repetition of previously established material.
- No contradictions between lessons.
- Natural conceptual connections between related lessons.

Each Stage maintains shared terminology, source records, and relevant continuity information.

Individual lessons reference shared records rather than duplicating them.

## 8. Production Architecture

### Stage-Level Preparation

Before producing a Stage:

1. Review its lesson sequence.
2. Map prerequisite relationships.
3. Define major concepts and their ownership.
4. Identify shared terminology and examples.
5. Identify reusable primary sources.
6. Define boundaries between neighboring lessons.

### Lesson-Level Production

Each lesson independently completes:

1. Research.
2. Source verification.
3. Lesson outline.
4. Full manuscript.
5. Necessary visuals and interactions.
6. Numerical verification where applicable.
7. Internal quality checks.
8. Academic review package.

## 9. Internal Files and PO Deliverables

Claude maintains production records in the project repository.

Potential internal files:

- Lesson brief and outline
- Source verification record
- Shared Stage source register
- Numerical verification script
- Visual and interaction proposal
- Batch progress tracker

Not every lesson requires every file.

### Standard PO Deliverable

Each lesson produces one consolidated Markdown file:

**Sxx-xx 学术审阅包.md**

Required contents:

A. Lesson information:
- ID and title
- Core question
- Learning objectives
- Prerequisites
- Teaching approach
- Teaching-body character count

B. Complete manuscript.

C. Concise audit summary:
- Important claims and source references
- Main primary sources
- Numerical verification results
- Unresolved evidence issues
- Important assumptions
- Cross-lesson consistency

D. Visual and interaction summary:
- Actual visuals and their purposes
- Actual interactions and their purposes
- Omit this section when unnecessary

Additional source files or scripts are supplied only when the PO or reviewer needs them.

The objective is to minimize routine review overhead without reducing academic transparency.

## 10. Academic Acceptance and Freeze

Every lesson passes four gates:

**Gate 1 — Research Complete**

Relevant research, sources, and numerical evidence are ready.

**Gate 2 — Manuscript Complete**

The complete teaching material and necessary visuals are produced and internally checked.

**Gate 3 — Independent Academic Review**

Review:
- Factual accuracy
- Source reliability
- Financial mechanism correctness
- Mathematical accuracy
- Teaching effectiveness
- Language quality
- Cross-lesson consistency

**Gate 4 — PO Approval**

The Product Owner decides whether to approve or request revisions.

Approved lessons become frozen.

### Reopening Rules

Frozen lessons may be reopened for:

- Substantive factual errors
- Materially outdated data or regulations
- Unsupported claims
- Significant cross-lesson contradictions
- Explicit PO requests

Do not repeatedly rewrite approved lessons for minor stylistic preferences.

## 11. Exceptions and Change Control

This standard defines defaults, not mechanical restrictions.

Claude may adapt:

- Chapter count
- Narrative format
- Mathematical depth
- Number of examples
- Number of visuals
- Interactive formats
- Lesson length

When an exception is needed:

1. Identify the relevant standard.
2. Explain why the normal approach is unsuitable.
3. Explain the educational benefit.
4. Request PO approval when the exception changes an important production requirement.

Do not independently weaken academic or numerical verification requirements.

## 12. Governance

- The 11-Stage, 61-lesson curriculum remains frozen.
- This standard applies to all future lessons.
- Stage-level preparation precedes mass lesson production.
- Production files remain internally maintained.
- Routine PO review uses one consolidated Markdown package per lesson.
- Git commits, pushes, and deployments require explicit PO authorization.
- Do not modify unrelated lessons, curriculum architecture, or website components without authorization.

**Final Principle**

Standardize production quality and academic accountability.

Preserve creative freedom in teaching structure and knowledge presentation.

The purpose of this standard is to make MarketPilot scalable without making its lessons repetitive.
